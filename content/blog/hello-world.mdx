I rebuilt my site. Figured the first post might as well be about the thing
you're reading it on.

The stack is boring in the good way: Next.js 16, React 19 with the compiler on,
Tailwind v4, Motion for animation, Shiki for code, and a few shadcn and Base UI
pieces so I'm not rebuilding a dropdown for the hundredth time. Nothing here
would surprise you.

What I actually spent time on is the stuff you don't notice until it's missing.

Try moving between a couple of pages. The content blurs and fades while the
header stays put. That's the View Transitions API, and getting it to feel right,
fast enough to not annoy but slow enough to register, took more fiddling than I'd
like to admit. If you keep reduced motion on, it stays out of your way.

Themes get the same attention. Dark and light each have their own code
highlighting, and you can flip between them without touching the mouse. Hit D
or M.

Then there's the stuff that never makes a changelog. Easing curves. Where a
border sits. How much room things get to breathe. On its own none of it matters.
Together it's the whole difference between a site that feels built and one that
feels assembled.

That's the tour. I'll write about actual things here soon.
